Where every dollar goes

100% of everything you put toward the game goes into what's declared below.

Art, servers, and the tools to build Elusion — and nothing else. I don't take a wage from the project, not a cent. The one exception is the hot-cocoa tip up top, which is a personal thank-you to me — and it is still on the ledger, on its own line, marked personal, because a total that quietly left something out would defeat the point of having one. Everything else not yet spent is held in the open for the next piece of art. The running total is below, kept honest and updated as it comes in; if a number ever looks off, email me and I'll walk you through it.

Art I'd love to fund

Art is the one thing I can't code. Every character and every corner of this world is drawn by hand, a piece at a time, and I pay for it out of my own pocket — that's how it has worked from the start and how it will keep working. This isn't a charity and nobody owes me anything. But people keep asking where the money would actually go, so here it is, to the dollar.

  • A character$200
  • An enemy$200
  • A prop$100

Real prices, not estimates — what a piece actually costs me with Ahvassa. Anything larger than these — a raid boss, a whole tileset — is quoted with him rather than listed, because I'm not going to publish a number I'd have to walk back.

Two ways to do it, and both are good ones. Send it through PayPal and say which piece — it gets commissioned in my name, so it goes exactly where it's needed and arrives in the game properly licensed. The shout-out is yours: your name on the wall above, and the artist hears whose it was.

Or hire Ahvassa yourself — genuinely, please do. He works hard for a lot of clients and Elusion is only one of them; more work coming his way is good for him, and that's good by me. Commission him for anything you like and just mention where you heard about him.

One thing only if the piece is for the game: talk to me before you order it. Every asset in Elusion carries a rights assignment to Elusion Studios, and that gets agreed with him up front rather than discovered afterwards, when the art is already drawn and in the build. It takes one message to get right and it is genuinely awkward to fix later. Anything you commission for yourself is yours and none of my business.
