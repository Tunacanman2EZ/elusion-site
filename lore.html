Beyond the Portal

A brief telling of where Elusion begins — and how much deeper it goes.

It starts in town, where the streets are safe and the coin is honest. Then there's the portal. No one agrees on what it is or where it goes — only that it's open, and that it pulls. Step through, and the world past it does not welcome you: the farther you go, the harder it gets — stranger ground, meaner things, less room for a single mistake.

Seven Lands

The world past the portal is made of seven lands, and each one has an element running through everything that lives there. Light and wind are the gentlest, and the first places anyone learns to fight. Water and ice come next, then earth, then fire. Deepest of all is dark, where even the slimes are dangerous. Every land pays back what it costs you — iron gear from the first two, then jade, cobalt and amethyst, and from the dark, rarely, ember.

The Crowned

The first great thing past the portal is the Crowned Behemoth — a beholder: one great eye crowned like a king, ringed with lesser eyes on restless stalks. It does not chase. It does not need to. It hangs in the air and turns the ground itself against you — rings of spikes that erupt where you're about to stand, walls of them that close the room to a single gap. Every eye watches a different death. Read the telegraphs and live; miss one and don't. And it is only Layer 1.

It wears more than one crown. Six of them are bound to an element — light, wind, water, ice, earth and fire — and each colors the Behemoth's patterns and its patience: the same eye seen through six different lights. The seventh crown is bound to nothing at all, and that one is the strongest of them.

The Things Between

The way down is not empty. Slimes that split when struck, sprites that spit fire and lightning, mages that raise thorns from the dirt and snipers that punish a careless step — six families of them, in every land's element. Clear them, and the world gives back: loot, coin, pets that fight at your side, and quiet things to do between fights — a fishing line, a cook-fire, a fair trade with another player.

What You Carry Out

No two pieces of gear are quite alike. Every piece that drops — and every piece the shop sells — is rolled when it's made: a little stronger here, a little weaker there. Once in a hundred, a piece comes out Perfect, every number at its best, and it glows gold to prove it.

Armour carries something of the land it came from. Every piece is attuned to one of the seven elements and turns aside a share of that element's harm — and pieces that share an element add up, until a full set of fire-touched plate takes half the sting out of a fire sprite's spit. Weapons never carry an element. What you hit for is your own strength and your own steel.

And somewhere out there are the mythic weapons: a falling Meteorite for the mage, a Double Axe for the warrior, a stick of Dynamite for the tank. Any monster might be carrying one. Most never are. When one turns up, the whole realm hears about it.

Coin and the Kingdom

Every coin in Elusion is real — earned, spent, and tracked on a server no client can lie to. Wealth can't be conjured; it comes from somewhere, and it can leave you just as fast. Fall in a fight and accept it, and you lose what you carried — the gold, the bag, the gear on your back. Only what you bank is safe, unless you pay to be brought back. Money you can lose is money that means something.

And the kingdom takes its cut. Every trade between players is taxed — but the tax isn't pocketed by anyone, it's burned. The gold leaves the world entirely, and that is the whole point: it's the only thing that keeps coin scarce and worth carrying, where a fee that simply changed hands would leave the realm exactly as rich as before. The shops work the same way — buy a potion or a blade and the coin doesn't line a merchant's purse, it's gone from the world for good, the everyday sink that keeps gold flowing out as fast as you earn it. Sell back what you've outgrown and the shop pays a twentieth of its worth — enough to clear a bag, never enough to beat the hunt. So spending isn't loss — it's a contribution. The Kingdom Board keeps the tally: not what you hoard, but what you've given the realm, ranked for all to see. Whoever has fed the kingdom most sits at the top of it.

You Are Not Alone Out There

Other adventurers walk the same fields you do. You see them as they move, fight beside them against the same monsters, and each walk away with your own loot — what you earn is yours, and what they earn is theirs. Form a guild, keep your friends close, trade what you don't need. And the realm keeps count: every monster felled is written down, by name, along with who has felled the most of them.

Into the Deep

Beating the Crowned Behemoth is not the end — it's the way down. Past it the world keeps going, and stops being a solo climb. The deep doesn't play fair: its monsters hit harder, move smarter, and never come one at a time. These are raids — fights no single player walks out of, that demand a party who read the room, spend cooldowns like currency, and keep each other alive. Every layer cleared feeds the kingdom's score. Go deeper, stay efficient, come back richer — or don't come back.

Your Part in It

You arrive as one of four: a Warrior who closes the distance, a Mage who never wants it closed, a Healer whose light comes ten times a second, or a Tank who simply refuses to fall. However you're built, the portal asks the same question four ways: how far down can you get, and can you get back?

The world is still being written. This is where it stands today.
