Model & assumptions

  • The run. The arena's gates open in waves — Light alone, then Wind and Water together, then Ice, Earth and Fire together — and its door leads to the Crowned Behemoth's room. Health, every boss's hit (its spike, its swing at 45/34 of it, a stalker's pillar at 22/34) and each element's telegraph speed are the game's. Faster telegraphs (Light, Wind) land more often at the same dodging; slower ones (Ice, Earth) less.
  • Holding. A boss with a player in reach swings instead of casting. With the tank holding, every boss swings at the tank — and at a sword warrior beside it — and the tank's aura hits every boss standing on it. Spread out, the bosses cast their spike patterns at the whole party instead. Stalkers come out from the second phase either way and hunt whoever the boss is on.
  • Your damage. hit = round((class base + weapon) × skills × gear %), the game's arithmetic: skills add 1% a level for attack and for magic, agility shortens every cooldown, gear adds its damage percent. Weapons hit at the middle of their roll. The Double Axe spins in place for a swing a second with no slash wave and no mana; the Meteorite casts twice one time in ten; a stick of Dynamite is four aura ticks, thrown once a second.
  • What gets through. The defense tier, then armour (armour ÷ (armour + 200)), then resistance to that boss's element, each off what the one before left — the game's order. Nobody heals: the Healer is pure offense, health and mana do not come back mid-fight, and potions (the best your level can drink) are taken below 40% health or when a cast can't be paid for. A downed player is out for the rest of the run — a revive lands in town.
  • Not in it. Pets, puddles, the Crowned's dim rings, and anyone standing in the wrong place. A deterministic expected-value model, attacking 85% of the time, of a game still in development.
